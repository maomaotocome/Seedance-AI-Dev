# Competitor pricing, "unlimited" economics and sample-video research

Research date: 2026-10-03. Read-only web research for seedanceai.dev.

**Source labels used throughout**

- **[1st]** = read first-hand on 2026-10-03 from the competitor's own page (raw HTML fetched and text-extracted).
- **[1st-sum]** = the competitor's own page, but read through an automated page summariser on 2026-10-03 (less reliable for exact numbers).
- **[2nd]** = secondary source (press, blog, another vendor's comparison table), read 2026-10-03.
- **[calc]** = my arithmetic from first-hand numbers; the inputs are in the same row.
- **[n/a]** = could not be fetched or not published; reason given.

USD per second is always `plan price / plan credits x credits per second`. Where a site does not publish the resolution behind its per-second rate, that is stated; do not compare those rows as if they were 720p.

### B2. How they afford it (pattern across the rows above)

1. **Time windows, not months.** 2-35 days, usually bundled into an annual prepay so one year of cash funds a few weeks of heavy use. Artlist was the only seller of 365 days and it lasted about a week.
2. **Resolution and length caps.** 480p-only 5s (OpenArt), 720p with 8-30s ceilings priced in steps (Creaa: $299 for 8s up to $999 for 30s).
3. **Queue as the real meter.** Slow standard queue, one active job (Unify), priority that drops if you only use unlimited (Topview). Throughput, not a counter, limits consumption.
4. **Hard caps under the label.** Artlist's 2,550 s/day; fair-use and account-review clauses everywhere.
5. **Web-only, no API/automation, no reference-video inputs** to exclude the most expensive jobs and resellers.
6. **Unlimited as an upsell to credit mode**, which stays faster and is always offered beside it.

### B3. What a small reseller paying about $0.14/s at 480p would risk [calc]

| Scenario (480p, concurrency 1, $0.14/s) | Seconds generated | API cost |
|---|---|---|
| One user, 10 clips of 5s per day for 30 days | 1,500 | $210 |
| One user, 1 hour of queue time per day (about 30 five-second clips) for 30 days | 4,500 | $630 |
| One job always running at roughly 1:1 render speed, 8 h/day for 7 days | about 200,000 | about $28,000 |
| Artlist-style cap of 2,550 s/day for 30 days | 76,500 | $10,710 |

- At $0.14/s a $49 plan breaks even at 350 seconds, i.e. 70 five-second clips. A motivated user passes that on day one.
- A pay-per-second API bill has no ceiling and is due immediately; Artlist, with far more capital and negotiated rates, could not hold the offer for a week.
- A reseller has no control over upstream queue priority, so the main throttle used by Topview/Unify (a slow lane) is not available; the only levers are a hard daily second cap, clip length, and 480p. Once a daily cap is published the product is a credit plan with a different label.
- Reputational and legal exposure: withdrawal produces press ("bait-and-switch" framing in CineD and others), chargebacks and refund demands. This also conflicts with the repo rule on truthful product claims.
- Unlimited attracts exactly the automation/resale users the big platforms write clauses against, and a small site has no abuse team.

Conclusion: do not sell unlimited Seedance 2.5. If a volume offer is wanted, sell a large fixed bucket of 480p seconds at a visible per-second price, or a short capped "draft pass" where the cap is the headline.

## What surprised us

1. **Several "peer" domains are not real Seedance 2.5 sellers.** seedance.ai states it does not provide the Seedance model at all; seedance2pro.com's pricing copy describes Seedance 1.5/1.0; seedance2-video.com sells only 2.0; seedance-ai.com is a parked domain for sale; seedance-25.art's create page still carries a "Coming soon" label for 2.5.
2. **Best-plan prices at peers ($0.12-$0.15/s) sit at or below the 480p API cost we pay ($0.14/s)** and well below the $0.315 720p cost. Either those rates are for 480p, the sites buy cheaper upstream, or annual breakage funds it. Topview openly sells 720p at $0.12/s on its $600 annual plan.
3. **Entry prices are five to ten times higher than best-plan prices on the same site** (seedance.tv $1.17 vs $0.28; Seavid $1.80 vs $0.30). The spread is the business model.
4. **Dreamina, the first-party seller, advertises $0.035/s at 720p** for a $1.50 first month. Competing on price against the official product is not viable.
5. **No small peer sells unlimited video**, and no peer gives a free Seedance 2.5 generation. Free tiers are one 480p 5s clip on a "Mini" model.
6. **Artlist's "unlimited" already had a 2,550 s/day cap and still collapsed in about a week.**
7. **seedance2ai.io attaches plan perks (priority queue, commercial licence, model discount) to one-time packs** and sells a $3.99 trial pack, a cheaper paid on-ramp than anyone else's.
8. **Annual credits delivered all at once** (seadance.io, seedance.ai, Seavid yearly, Topview Business) is common among small sites, which front-loads API liability.
9. **Nobody shows a real 2.0 vs 2.5 video comparison or structured settings next to samples**, and native-audio samples are all muted. That is an open presentation gap.
10. **Refund terms are tight and fee-bearing**: 3 days and 8% (seedance.tv), 7 days with unused-credit conditions and 3% (seedance2ai.io), annual plans excluded.
